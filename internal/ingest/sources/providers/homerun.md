**Homerun traps** (`homerun`, a Dutch ATS for small and mid-sized employers at `<tenant>.homerun.co` or the employer's own domain; live-verified 2026-10-25 — 874 tenant labels in five Common Crawl snapshots, 623 serve a feed, 515 have openings, 2,499 vacancies, the largest `douglas` at 106; ~15% technical by title):

- **The listing is one open Atom feed per tenant, `feed.homerun.co/<tenant>`**, whatever domain the career site lives on: every open vacancy with its full body (`<description>`; `<content>` is only the teaser), `location>name`, `type>name`, `department>name` and `<updated>` (the publication time, `2006-01-02 15:04:05`). The career page itself renders its list in the browser, so it is no listing.
- **The feed's location is the employer's NAME for an office** ("Douglas Hoofdkantoor - Nijmegen", "Preston Palace", "Hybride", "Remote", "Other"). The vacancy page's JobPosting carries that office's postal address and ISO country on ~65% of pages; the rest carry none or an empty ld+json block (an office without an address). So a NEW vacancy costs one page request, and the page is only ENRICHMENT: a refused page stores the feed entry as it stands, never a marker. TELECOMMUTE (47 of 1,332 JobPostings) is read as remote; the office name stays in `Location` beside the page's town, so the parser still reads "Hybride".
- **The feed keeps vacancies whose page is gone**: 62 of 2,499 entries (2.5%) named a page that answers 404. A 404 on the page drops the vacancy; a stored vacancy is never re-fetched, so one that dies later stays open until the feed drops it.
- **A gone tenant's feed redirects to `404.homerun.co`, which answers 404** (`ErrBoardGone`). The tenant HOST is no signal: 115 tenants whose site redirects to `404.homerun.co/working_at/<tenant>` still serve live vacancies on their feed. DNS answers every label.
- **Every feed and vacancy page is served by one AWS origin, and it refuses by RATE**: an unpaced harvest of 893 feeds from fsn drew 429s within 8 seconds and then a 403 on every request from that address for over an hour. Every request the adapter makes draws on one provider-wide budget (`homerunRequestInterval`), and `harvest-boards homerun` must run with `-pace 1` or slower. **2/s was not slow enough from host2 either**: on 2026-10-25..27 five of six scheduled runs were refused 60-220 s in (~120-440 feed requests) and then sent a refused request every 500 ms until they ran out of boards — 138-215 failed boards a run; the block had lifted by the next run, and the rotating gateway was served throughout. So the budget is 1/s and the first 403/429 on the direct IP moves the rest of the run, and the refused request, to `SOURCES_ROTATING_PROXY_URL` (`homerunEgress`, wired by `ApplyProxyEgress`; never the fixed exit). Without the gateway the first refusal stops the run's Homerun traffic: later feeds fail unsent with `errHomerunStopped`, and a new vacancy whose page went unrequested is deferred, not stored without its address.
