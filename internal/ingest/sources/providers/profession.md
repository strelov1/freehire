**Profession.hu traps** (all verified live 2026-09-03, over the whole 770-URL IT slice unless a smaller sample is named):

- **The board is one of the platform's own CATEGORY SITEMAPS, and the two IT ones are the whole onboarding.** Profession.hu is one central Hungarian catalogue with no per-employer tenancy; it publishes 23 category sitemaps holding ~12k postings, of which `itdev` (508) and `itops` (262) are IT. That is the AppliTrack/EDJOIN lesson applied before it cost anything: `classify.ConfirmedNonTech` was written against tech-company ATS boards and leaks on a general-population board, so the platform's own facet picks the slice. Over 128 live postings from those two categories it rejected **zero**, and a full crawl of both confirmed it — 709 postings ingested, none rejected, none body-less, none nameless. The same trust now also sets `Job.IsTechHint` on every posting `detail()` returns (issue #2601): the title dictionary resolves a category for only about half of these postings, and before this signal existed a posting it missed sat with `is_tech` unknown forever — `EnqueuePendingJobs`'s `is_tech IS TRUE` gate never queued it for enrichment, and it never became searchable.
- **The other 21 categories are REFUSED, and `list()` says so before it reads anything.** They do hold technical postings — a live audit of all 21 on 2026-09-06 found roughly **30** — but nothing in a TITLE separates them from their neighbours, and that was measured rather than argued. The audit crawled all 21 behind a URL-slug prefilter and a title check, both reading `internal/dict/classify`: **11,403 listed → 227 passed the cheap gate → 159 stored**. What those 159 resolved through is the whole finding:

  | resolved through | n | what it actually was |
  |---|---|---|
  | `data_analytics` (bare `analyst`) | 45 | VAT, credit control, valuation, lease administration |
  | `project_management` | 23 | construction, manufacturing, HR payroll |
  | `business_analysis` | 13 | |
  | `design` + `creative` | 13 | concept artists, illustrators, a furniture showroom designer |
  | `product` | 11 | |
  | `qa` | 10 | a chemical plant's and a pharma plant's quality managers |
  | `solutions_engineering` | 5 | "Sales Engineer" |
  | genuinely technical crafts | ~29 | |
  | **`classify.IsTech(title)`** | **17** | |

  **The gate was not tuned badly — it used the wrong predicate.** `vocab.TechCategories` names the job families of a TECH COMPANY, `product`/`project_management`/`design`/`creative`/`business_analysis` included; that is right on a software employer's board and wrong on a general-population one. Making it work needs three corrections at once — a craft-only predicate, an exclusion for the bare `analyst`/`qa`/`architect`, and two Hungarian aliases the audit disproved (`hálózatüzemeltető` matched three gas fitters, bare `tesztelő` a pump tester) — and 30 postings do not buy a mechanism nobody can keep correct. **If you are about to propose this again, the numbers above are the thing to beat.**
- **`cmd/harvest-boards profession` DISCOVERS its boards and probes them cheaply** — `professionProber`, using the exported `sources.ProfessionCategorySitemaps` / `sources.ProfessionSitemapPostings`. The sitemap index IS the authoritative board list, so there is no seed file and nothing to keep in step by hand; and counting a category's sitemap costs one request, where the `adapterProber` fallback would crawl every posting in all 23 categories to answer the same question. The sitemap URL shape stays in `profession.go` alone: a prober that rebuilt it would be a second answer to "where is a category's sitemap", and the two would disagree silently the first time the platform moved one. A discovered board is labelled `Profession.hu — <category>`; `cmd/add-board --rename` gives it the platform's own wording, and nothing about the crawl depends on it.
- **THE PLATFORM MISHANDLES A REUSED HTTP CONNECTION, and that is what took the crawl down between 2026-09-04 and 2026-09-06.** Over one pooled connection it answers the first request or two and then returns a bare `EOF` for everything after — and one of the answers it *does* give can be **truncated**: measured from the production host on 2026-09-06, `sitemap-listings-itdev-hu.xml` came back as **379 bytes where the document is 76,403**, which parses cleanly as a sitemap that has lost its postings. A dedicated connection per request answered all five test URLs in full. So `profession` is wired through `singleUseConnSource` / `sources.NewSingleUseConnClient` (`safehttp.NewTransportWithoutKeepAlive`), and its `harvest-boards` prober reads over its own copy of that client rather than the run's shared one — the prober reads the same sitemaps, so it meets the same wall. **Do not diagnose this as pacing or as a rate limit.** Three explanations were tried live and all three left the failure exactly where it was: 25-second spacing between requests, `CloseIdleConnections()` between retries, and `ForceAttemptHTTP2: false`. Our three retries are 500 ms apart and reuse the same pool, so a retry can never recover it. `curl` — a new process, hence a new connection, every time — never reproduces it, which is what makes this look for hours like the platform blocking our IP.
- **THE SITEMAP INDEX IS READ ONCE PER RUN, and that is load rather than tidiness.** `resolveSubSitemap` re-reads the index on every call — the right shape for a provider with one board, and invisible while this crawl was two categories. With 23 it is 23 requests for one document, and **the platform does not tolerate it**: measured live on 2026-09-06 it answered the first request and then closed the connection (`EOF` after 3 attempts) on every one that followed, which arrives downstream as every category being unreachable and looks nothing like the crawl asking too often. Both sides now memoize — `professionIndex` on the adapter (one crawl) and `professionCategoryIndex` on the prober (one harvest, whose `probeAll` runs candidates concurrently). Only a SUCCESSFUL read is cached, so one transient failure does not decide the remaining boards, and the lock is deliberately held across the fetch so concurrent boards wait for the one request instead of racing to make their own. **Pace a manual harvest** (`harvest-boards -pace`); this platform rate-limits harder than the ATS fleet does.
- **`jobLocationType: "TELECOMMUTE"` means NOT STRICTLY ONSITE**, and it is set on 48% of the slice. The page shows those same postings as **`Hibrid`**, and the badge above the address is the ONLY thing separating hybrid from remote — the ld+json spends one flag on both. Worse, a posting carrying that flag has **no `jobLocation` at all**, so reading only the structured block loses the city on half the crawl. Badge and flag agreed on 128 of 128, so the page's address line is read for both. The third badge, `Országos lefedettség` ("nationwide coverage"), is neither a work mode nor a place.
- **The address line's postal code and street number are what stop the location dictionary.** `"Budapest"` resolves; `"1087 Budapest, Kerepesi út 21."` resolves nothing, because the code is glued to the city inside one comma-token. Dropping digit runs took a 128-posting sample from 61 resolved cities to 92, and the live crawl lands at 78-80%. The street name is left in — it resolves to nothing, and removing it would mean guessing which token is the city.
- **A taken-down posting answers 200, not 404 or 410**: the URL redirects to the platform's category listing, whose page carries no JobPosting block. **61 of the 770 listed URLs (7.9%)** are already in that state, so a body-less read is the ordinary case rather than a failure — the posting is skipped and never stored. The sitemap is therefore not a liveness list.
- **The ld+json `description` flattens the posting's bullet lists into one run-on paragraph.** The page carries the same text in the sections the employer typed it into (`#tasks` 100%, `#requirements` 98%, `#offer` 94%, `#other` 69%, `#workplace_extras` 17%), list markup intact, so the body is read from those. The section HEADING is the employer's own words — the same `#tasks` is headed "Feladatok", "A pozíció leírása", "Responsibilities" and "MI LESZ A FELADATOD?" — so it identifies nothing and is carried through as text. `#c_info` (the employer's standing blurb, the `employerOverview` call edjoin and gusto make) and `#contact_info` (where a postal address or a recruiter's mail lands) are excluded.
- **`occupationalCategory` is a good SELECTOR and a bad LABEL**, so it picks the board and is NOT mapped onto a category. Its 20 IT leaves were read against the titles of all 709 live postings: the largest, "AI és Automatizáció" (124), holds Senior Java Developer, AWS Cloud Engineer and a Vice President of Government Sales; "IT tanácsadó, Elemző, Auditor" (73) holds a Backend Engineer, an Energy Economist and a Technical Writer. Even the honest leaves lose by being read — a structured `Job.Category` takes precedence over the title dictionary (`jobderive`'s category precedence), and the dictionary already resolves **47%** of this slice, so the best map this vocabulary supports added a category to 132 postings while **flattening 76** the dictionary had placed more precisely (`backend`, `fullstack`, `frontend`, `mobile` → `software_engineering`). Two of its leaves would additionally have mapped onto `vocab.NonTechCategories` members (`IT support, Helpdesk` → `support`, `IT, fejlesztési vezető` → `management`), flipping `is_tech` off for 80 postings the platform's own IT facet had selected.
- **The country is stated in one of two mutually exclusive places and in three spellings.** A sited posting carries `jobLocation.address.addressCountry` (`"HU"`); a not-strictly-onsite one carries `applicantLocationRequirements` instead, naming the country as `"Magyarország"` or `"Hungary"`. The endonym is now in the location dictionary's country names beside `brasil` and `türkiye`. A posting sited abroad (`Külföld`) names no country the dictionary knows and correctly yields none.
- **`employmentType` is multi-valued and mixes a CONTRACT FORM with a SCHEDULE.** `"Alkalmazotti jogviszony"` (employee status) is 600 of 709 and names no schedule at all — the `ukgready` `employee_type` trap in a second language. Only `Teljes munkaidő`/`Részmunkaidő`/`Diákmunka` map. `experienceRequirements` beside it IS a closed picklist, six values across the whole slice, and maps cleanly.
- **The slice publishes no salary at all**: `data-item-variant` read `"salary confidential"` on every one of 128 sampled postings and no ld+json field carries an amount. Nothing to map.
- **No metering observed**: two full board crawls hydrated 709 posting pages at the shared 8-way detail fan-out (~5 req/s) with no refusals — 458 postings in 95s, 251 in 50s. No pacer is wired; `pacer.go` is where one would go. Note that **the origin refuses some residential and non-EU egress** (it times out from a developer machine while answering the prod crawl host in 0.4s), so validate a board from the crawl host rather than locally.
