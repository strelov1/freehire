**Hacker News traps** (`hackernews`; verified live 2026-09-14):

- **Find the thread by the `whoishiring` account's author tag, never by free text.** `search_by_date?tags=story,author_whoishiring` lists that account's stories newest-first; a free-text query is RANKED and an unrelated recent story containing the words outranks the thread once it ages (career-ops hit exactly this). The account also posts "Who wants to be hired?" and "Freelancer?" threads every month, so the title is checked against `hackernewsHiringTitle`.
- **A crawl reads the two newest hiring threads whole** (this month's, still filling, and last month's, still live in the first weeks) — one `items/<id>` request each, ~500 KB, every top-level comment with its HTML in one document; 417 postings from the two live threads at the time this was measured. Replies are discussion and are not read. Any thread failing fails the crawl: the provider is `fullCatalog`, so a post from a thread that aged out of the window closes by source, which nothing else would do for a boardless feed.
- **The post format is a convention, not a schema.** The first paragraph (up to the first unclosed `<p>`) is read as the "Company | Role | Location | ..." header; a post with fewer than two pipe segments names no employer this catalogue could file it under and is DROPPED. The location is the first later segment that is not a commitment word, a URL or a salary. The apply link is the first anchor in the post, falling back to the comment's permalink; the whole comment is the body. Segment order varies in practice (some posts lead with the location) and that noise is left to the title dictionaries rather than guessed at.
- **A header opening with a ROLE instead of an employer shifts every later segment by one** ("Senior Software Engineer, Frontend | New York, NY (In-Office) | Full-time" would otherwise file the role as the company and the location as the role) — detected by a seniority word paired with a role noun in the first segment, and dropped rather than corrected, since nothing in that shape recovers the real employer. A seniority word ALONE is not evidence (`Lead Bank`, `Chief Industries` are real employers), so only the pair triggers the drop.
- **The salary-segment skip decodes a RUNE, not a byte.** € and £ are multi-byte in UTF-8; slicing a segment's first byte to check it against `$€£` cuts into the middle of a multi-byte symbol and never matches, so a €/£ salary segment would otherwise be mistaken for the location. `startsWithCurrencySymbol` decodes the first rune instead.
