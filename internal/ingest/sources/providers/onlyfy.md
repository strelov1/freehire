**onlyfy traps** (`onlyfy`, onlyfy by XING — formerly prescreen — at `<board>.onlyfy.jobs`; live-verified 2026-10-25 across the 143 hosts Common Crawl names on `onlyfy.jobs` and `jobbase.io` — 74 distinct live sites, 1,788 postings, mostly Germany and Austria):

- **The listing is an HTML fragment**, `<site>/candidate/job/ajax_list?display_length=<n>&page=<p>`, the one the career site's "load more" button reads. Its pager script states the board's posting count (`$('#pageInfoTotalCount').text(185)`), so the walk must reach it or the board fails (`fullBoardListing`); a fragment without that script is not a listing. A page past the end, and an empty board, answer a "There are currently no vacant positions." row under HTTP 200; an unknown site answers 404 (`ErrBoardGone`).
- **`<board>.jobbase.io` is the old domain** and redirects to `<board>.onlyfy.jobs`; a renamed site redirects to its new name (`lloyd-fonds-ag` → `laiqon-ag`). The harvest prober skips a candidate whose listing redirects to another site name, so one site cannot become two boards; the destination is a candidate of its own.
- **A posting is read from its schema.org JobPosting** (`<site>/job/<id>`, redirected to a language-prefixed path) when the page renders one — about half the sites. A site with its own job template renders none (de-cix), and the first paced crawl left 1,422 of 3,004 postings unreadable on that alone; there the body is the platform's own job-ad view, `<site>/job/show/<id>/full`, whose `#jobAdContainer` every template shares, and the title, place and date are the listing row's. The listing names no employer, so the harvest prober reads the first posting's `hiringOrganization`.
- **Every site shares one platform edge that answers 429 to the run's aggregate rate.** The first unpaced crawl (125 boards, boards and posting pages fanned out) had 37 boards fail their listing on 429 and 1,544 posting pages come back unreadable; the adapter is now paced to ~3 req/s (`onlyfyRequestInterval`).
- **`jobLocationType: TELECOMMUTE` is not a remote signal.** It sits on a petrol-station clerk and a construction engineer alike: of 26 sampled postings carrying it, 16 mention no remote work anywhere in the body. The adapter leaves the work mode to the description parser.
