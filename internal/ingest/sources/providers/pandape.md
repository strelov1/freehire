**Pandapé traps** (`pandape`, InfoJobs Brasil's ATS; live-verified 2026-10-23 from the fsn host, every one of the 3,347 tenants the sitemap names):

- **A board is the tenant label** of `<tenant>.pandape.infojobs.com.br`, one career site per employer — the gupy/hiringroom shape, not an aggregator. The same tenant answers on `<tenant>.pandape.catho.com.br` and `pandape.com.br` with the identical listing, so `atsboard` maps all three domains to one board and the adapter reads only the infojobs host. Staffing agencies (randstad-brasil, adecco, manpowergroup, gigroup1) are tenants too and post under their OWN name ("Randstad - Matriz" in every JobPosting sampled), so the board's company is the right attribution; there is no client name to recover.
- **Why not the sitemap.** robots.txt names a gzip sitemap index (115 shards, 113,583 URLs, 93,872 distinct vacancies, 3,347 tenants) and it would make a tempting boardless crawl, but it is not the catalogue: every tenant's own listing is a SUPERSET of its sitemap entries, and a large one. braspress lists 54 against 20 sitemap URLs, atacadao 2,119 against 976, randstad-brasil 3,827 against 3,033; across the fleet the listings carry **248,248** vacancies against the sitemap's 93,872 (38%). A sitemap-swept provider would miss most of the catalogue and, swept by source, close what it merely failed to list. 35 entries are host-less (`<loc>/Detail/3552612</loc>`), so they name no employer at all. The sitemap is used for DISCOVERY only (`cmd/harvest-boards pandape`).
- **The list is the career site's own pager, `POST /ListVacancies`**, form-encoded `PageNumber=<n>&PageSize=20`. It answers JSON: the page's cards as an HTML fragment (`view`), the tenant's total (`subtotal`) and `isLast`. `PageSize` above 20 is ignored. The walk ends only on `isLast` and fails unless the distinct vacancies reach the first page's `subtotal`; any page failing, an empty page before the end, and a stated total past the pager's 500-page window (see the LatAm section) all fail the crawl (`fullBoardListing`). An unknown tenant answers 404 with "A Página de Carreiras desta empresa não está mais disponível no Pandapé", wrapped as `ErrBoardGone`.
- **Cards are icon-keyed picklists**: `icon-location-pin-1` the place ("Guarulhos - SP", or "Todo Brasil" for a nationwide one), `icon-buildings`/`icon-building-house`/`icon-user-with-headphones` the work mode (Presencial / Híbrido / Home Office), `icon-sheet` the contract (Efetivo – CLT, Prestador de Serviços (PJ), Autônomo, Temporário, Estágio, Jovem Aprendiz, Outros). The vacancy page uses DIFFERENT icon names for the same facts, so facts are read from the card only.
- **The vacancy page (`/Detail/<id>`) may carry a schema.org JobPosting** with `datePosted` (no offset — read as Brasília time, UTC-3), `jobLocation.address` (locality, region, `addressCountry` BR → `Countries`), `employmentType` (`full-time`/`partial-time`, the SCHEDULE: a PJ contract still says `full-time`, so the card's contract wins) and, on about two thirds of postings, `baseSalary` with `unitText: MONTH`. Its `description` replaces every emoji with `?`, so the body is the page's own `#description` HTML plus its structured "Requisitos"/"Valorizado" sections. Since 2026-11-02, sampled Brazilian pages omit JSON-LD while retaining the vacancy body and identity. Without a decodable JobPosting, accept only a page whose hidden `input#HdnIdVacancy` matches the listed id and whose `#description .order-1` contains text; requirements alone are not a description. Keep the card fields and Brazilian country fallback, and leave absent structured fields unset. A 404 is a posting gone; robots disallows only `/Apply/`, which the adapter never requests.
- **`empregos` is not a tenant.** `empregos.pandape.infojobs.com.br` (10,484 sitemap URLs, 11% of the sitemap) is the platform's bucket for vacancies posted as "Empresa com nome confidencial": its root 404s while its `/Detail` pages answer. It has no listing to prove completeness against and names no employer, so `atsboard` declines it and the harvest never proposes it.
- **One origin, and it answers a burst with 403, not 429.** The shared client's per-host backoff only sees 429, so the adapter paces every request of a run through one limiter at 2/s: that rate sustained for ~40 minutes drew no refusal, while ~6-7/s for a minute drew a run of 403s on listings and details alike, which cleared within seconds. The limiter is per PROCESS, so concurrent shards multiply the rate — run the provider UNSHARDED. A full listing pass is ~14.3k pages, ~2 hours at that rate; a first crawl of a tenant hydrates at most 300 vacancies (`pandapeMaxNewPerBoard`, ~2.5 minutes), so the largest tenants converge over several runs. The ~41k card titles sampled hit `classify.ConfirmedNonTech` zero times, so no hydrated body is thrown away by the catalogue filter.
- **Onboarding** is `go run ./cmd/harvest-boards pandape` (discovers tenants from the sitemap, probes each career site's `/Vacancies` page for its name and open count, reports — held to the same 2 req/s whatever `-pace` says, so ~30 minutes: its first run went out unpaced at ~35 req/s, the 403s it drew were read as absent boards, 886 of 3,420 came back live against the 3,334 the survey measured, and the next run's sitemap fetch was refused outright) then the same with `--apply` (inserts `pending`), then `go run ./cmd/schedule-board --provider=pandape --manage --heavy --timeout=10800s --cadence=12h --notes=... --apply`. A tenant whose label is longer than a DNS label allows (two in the 2026-10-23 sitemap) cannot be reached and is skipped by the probe.

**Pandapé LatAm traps** (`pandapelatam`, Computrabajo's deployment of the same ATS; live-verified 2026-10-24):

- **Same software, separate provider.** `<tenant>.pandape.computrabajo.com` serves the identical `POST /ListVacancies` pager, cards and `/Detail/<id>` pages, so it is the same adapter (`pandapeDomain`). It is a SEPARATE tenant namespace — `netcall` 404s on the infojobs domain and `braspress` 404s here — which is why the jobgether harvest's 18 hosts all probed dead under `pandape`, and why it is its own provider: a board label alone does not say which domain it lives on, and the vacancy ids are a separate sequence (13.9M here, 3.8M in Brazil).
- **The edge filters on the User-Agent**, unlike the Brazilian one: measured 2026-10-24 from fsn, `freehire/0.1 (+https://freehire.me)` and curl's default get 403 on every path, while `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; freehire/0.1; +https://freehire.me)` is served — so the provider rides `compatibleUASource` and its harvest prober the same client (`probeClienter`). robots.txt disallows only `/Apply/`. The first harvest went out on the default client and read every tenant as a probe failure.
- **The JobPosting is a Brazilian template** (`templatedJobPosting`): every one sampled says `addressCountry: BR`, `currency: BRL` and `employmentType: partial-time` — a Lima call centre's full-time job paid in soles included — and drops the last letter of a locality with no region (`Ayacuch`, `Santiago Centr`). Some vacancy pages carry no JobPosting at all; there a page with a body is still a vacancy (`PostedAt` stays empty). Only `datePosted` and the salary bounds are read from it.
- **The country is the page's analytics props**, `window.dataLayerProps.country = 'Chile'` (an English name, through `location.NormalizeCountry`) — the tenant's country, on both the career site and every vacancy page. The place is the card's location; the work mode its picklist (`Presencial` / `Presencial y remoto` = hybrid / `Remoto`).
- **The currency comes from the country**, and only when the card's salary (`icon-wallet`) is written in that country's own symbol (`S/ 1,230` for Peru, `1.000.000 $` for Chile/Colombia/Argentina/Mexico/Uruguay); a card in `US$` keeps no salary. A country outside `pandapeLatamCurrencies` keeps no salary either — add it there when one appears.
- **The salary form is free text and the page never names a currency but the portal's.** Measured 2026-10-28, 311 of 1,318 open Argentine salaries were not figures: filler (`111.111 $`, `11.111 $ a 2.222.222 $`) and 2,000-33,000 ARS a month for full-time jobs (probably dollars or an hourly rate — but the page says `$` and `(Mensual bruto)`, nothing else, and the JobPosting says BRL). `pandapeLatamSalaryPlausible` drops a range with a repeated-digit bound or a monthly bound under a twentieth of its country's measured median; it never re-reads one as dollars. A country added to `pandapeLatamCurrencies` needs its median measured first. `salary_*_source` is not in `content_hash` and a stored posting is never re-read, so the rule reaches stored rows only through a refetch or an UPDATE applying the same rule.
- **Employment type is the card's schedule** (`icon-clock`: `Tiempo completo` / `Jornada completa` / `Jornada de N horas semanales` with N ≥ 40 → full time; `Tiempo parcial` / `Jornada part time` / `Por horas` → part time). The many contract wordings (indefinido, a plazo fijo, de obra o labor, …) are employment and say nothing about the schedule; only `Contrato civil por prestación de servicios` is a contract.
- **The pager serves 500 pages (10,000 vacancies) and no more**, on both domains: page 501 answers `{"isLast": true}` with no cards and no subtotal. A tenant stating more (activoschile 55,483, adecco-colombia 10,733) can never be listed whole, so its crawl fails on the first page and `harvest-boards` does not propose it.
- **Discovery is Common Crawl** (`*.pandape.computrabajo.com`): the domain has no sitemap of its own (its robots.txt names the Brazilian one). `www` redirects to the marketing site and `empleos` is the confidential bucket (root 404), so `atsboard` declines both. The harvest probes `/Vacancies`, not `/`: a tenant on the richer home template renders no counter on its home page.
